EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.

C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected

============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains

============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.

MATERIAL TO EVALUATE

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Stock Transfer and Redistribution

Scenario:
One location holds surplus stock approaching a risk of expiration while another location faces insufficient stock.

Objective:
Realign inventory between locations to reduce the risk of expiry and prevent stockout.

Operational information:
Stock analysis identifies excess quantity of an item at one facility and insufficient quantity at another facility. The responsible Supply function determines that redistribution is required. The source location must prepare the agreed quantity for collection or dispatch. The receiving location must receive and record the stock. Picking, packing, transport handover, receiving and stock-record updates must remain traceable.

Roles involved:
Supply Officer, Store Manager, Store Supervisor, Storekeeper, Store Helper/Warehouse Staff, transport/3PL personnel, receiving facility.

Constraints / decision conditions:
The redistribution quantity must be based on stock information and an authorized decision. Stock must remain traceable throughout the transfer. The movement should not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.

Expected outcome:
Inventory is redistributed from the surplus location to the shortage location and both locations' stock records correctly reflect the movement.


CANDIDATE SOP:

# 1. SOP Title

**Standard Operating Procedure: Stock Transfer and Redistribution Between Locations**

## 2. Purpose

To define the process for redistributing stock from a location holding surplus inventory at risk of expiration to a location with insufficient stock. The procedure aims to reduce expiry risk, prevent stockout, maintain traceability, and ensure accurate stock records at both locations.

## 3. Scope

This SOP applies to stock redistribution between:

- A **source location** holding excess stock; and
- A **receiving location** with insufficient stock.

It covers:

- Stock analysis and the redistribution decision;
- Confirmation of the transfer quantity;
- Picking and packing;
- Collection or dispatch;
- Transport handover;
- Receipt at the receiving location; and
- Stock-record updates at both locations.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Supply Officer

- Review stock information identifying excess stock at one location and insufficient stock at another.
- Determine whether redistribution is required.
- Ensure the redistribution quantity is based on available stock information and an authorized decision.
- Ensure the movement does not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
- Coordinate the redistribution with the relevant locations and transport personnel.

### 4.2 Store Manager

- Oversee transfer preparation at the source location.
- Confirm that the agreed quantity is prepared for collection or dispatch.
- Ensure transfer activities and stock-record updates remain traceable.

### 4.3 Store Supervisor

- Supervise picking, packing, and handover activities.
- Verify that the prepared stock corresponds to the agreed redistribution quantity.
- Ensure traceability is maintained during preparation and handover.

### 4.4 Storekeeper

- Pick and prepare the agreed stock quantity.
- Support verification, packing, handover, and stock-record updates.
- Maintain traceability of the stock throughout source-location activities.

### 4.5 Store Helper/Warehouse Staff

- Assist with picking, packing, movement, staging, and loading of the stock as directed.
- Preserve the identity and traceability of the stock during handling.

### 4.6 Transport/3PL Personnel

- Accept the stock at transport handover.
- Maintain the traceability of the transferred stock while it is in transport custody.
- Deliver the stock to the receiving location.

### 4.7 Receiving Facility

- Receive the transferred stock.
- Verify the received stock against the agreed transfer.
- Record the receipt and update the receiving location’s stock records.
- Report any discrepancy or traceability issue.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information

The process requires:

- Stock information showing excess quantity at the source location;
- Stock information showing insufficient quantity at the receiving location;
- Identification of the item to be redistributed;
- An authorized decision to redistribute the stock;
- The agreed redistribution quantity;
- Identification of the source and receiving locations;
- Information required to maintain traceability through picking, packing, transport handover, receipt, and stock-record updates; and
- Collection or dispatch arrangements.

## 6. Expected Outputs

- The agreed stock quantity is transferred from the surplus location to the shortage location.
- Expiry risk at the source location is reduced.
- Stockout risk at the receiving location is reduced.
- The movement does not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
- The transfer remains traceable through all handling stages.
- Source and receiving stock records correctly reflect the movement.
- Any discrepancy or deviation is identified and addressed before the process is considered complete.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Identify the Redistribution Need

1. Review stock information for the relevant item at both locations.
2. Confirm that:
   - The source location holds excess stock approaching a risk of expiration; and
   - The receiving location has insufficient stock.
3. The Supply Officer determines whether redistribution is required.

### 7.2 Determine and Authorize the Transfer Quantity

1. Determine the proposed redistribution quantity using the available stock information.
2. Confirm that transferring the proposed quantity will not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
3. Obtain or confirm the authorized decision for the redistribution.
4. Communicate the agreed item, quantity, source location, and receiving location to the personnel involved.
5. Do not prepare or move stock based solely on an unsupported quantity or an unauthorized decision.

### 7.3 Prepare Stock at the Source Location

1. The Store Manager or Store Supervisor coordinates preparation of the agreed quantity.
2. The Storekeeper, supported by the Store Helper/Warehouse Staff, identifies and picks the stock.
3. Verify that the picked item and quantity match the authorized redistribution decision.
4. Maintain traceability while the stock is picked, moved, and staged.
5. Pack the stock for collection or dispatch.
6. Keep the packed stock identifiable and traceable pending transport handover.

### 7.4 Complete Transport Handover

1. Make the prepared stock available for collection or dispatch.
2. At handover, the responsible source-location personnel and transport/3PL personnel confirm the stock being transferred.
3. Ensure the handover remains traceable, including the item and quantity transferred.
4. Transfer custody of the stock to the transport/3PL personnel.
5. Update the source location’s stock records to reflect the stock movement.
6. Preserve the relationship between the handover information and the corresponding stock-record update.

### 7.5 Transport the Stock

1. Transport/3PL personnel move the stock from the source location to the receiving location.
2. Maintain stock identity and traceability while the stock is in transport custody.
3. Deliver the stock to the identified receiving facility.

### 7.6 Receive and Verify the Stock

1. The receiving facility accepts the delivered stock.
2. Verify the received item and quantity against the agreed transfer and transport handover information.
3. Confirm that the stock remains traceable to the source-location transfer.
4. Identify and report any quantity difference, incorrect item, loss of traceability, or other deviation before completing the receipt.

### 7.7 Record Receipt and Reconcile Records

1. The receiving facility records the stock receipt.
2. Update the receiving location’s stock records by the quantity received and accepted.
3. Confirm that:
   - The source location’s records reflect the stock dispatched;
   - The receiving location’s records reflect the stock received; and
   - The records correspond to the traceable transfer information.
4. Reconcile any difference between the dispatched and received quantities before considering the transfer complete.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling

### 8.1 Proposed Transfer Would Create a Source Shortage

- Do not proceed with a quantity that would create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
- Refer the quantity for review by the responsible Supply function.
- Proceed only after an authorized redistribution quantity has been confirmed.

### 8.2 Quantity Is Not Authorized or Is Unclear

- Do not pick, dispatch, or receive the stock as a completed transfer until the agreed quantity is confirmed through an authorized decision.
- The Supply Officer must resolve the uncertainty and communicate the confirmed quantity.

### 8.3 Picking or Packing Discrepancy

- Stop preparation if the item or quantity does not match the agreed transfer.
- Recheck the stock information and authorized decision.
- Correct the discrepancy before transport handover.

### 8.4 Handover Discrepancy

- Do not complete the transport handover if the stock presented does not match the agreed item or quantity.
- The source-location personnel and transport/3PL personnel must identify the discrepancy.
- Correct the stock or confirm an authorized change before custody is transferred.

### 8.5 Receiving Discrepancy

If the receiving facility identifies a shortage, excess, incorrect item, or other mismatch:

- Record the actual quantity and item received.
- Maintain the stock separately identifiable while the discrepancy is being resolved where necessary to preserve traceability.
- Notify the relevant transfer participants.
- Reconcile the source dispatch information, transport handover information, physical receipt, and stock records.
- Do not treat the transfer as complete until the discrepancy has been addressed and the records reflect the resolved movement.

### 8.6 Loss of Traceability

- Stop the affected activity where traceability cannot be maintained or demonstrated.
- Identify the last point at which the stock movement was traceable.
- Reconcile the available picking, packing, handover, receiving, and stock-record information.
- Do not complete the transfer until traceability has been restored or the deviation has been addressed.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria

The stock transfer and redistribution process is complete only when:

- Redistribution was based on stock information and an authorized decision;
- The agreed quantity was prepared and dispatched from the source location;
- The movement did not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location;
- The stock was delivered to and accepted by the receiving facility;
- Picking, packing, transport handover, receiving, and record updates are traceable;
- The source location’s stock records reflect the dispatched quantity;
- The receiving location’s stock records reflect the received quantity; and
- All discrepancies or deviations have been addressed and the records correctly reflect the final stock movement.
