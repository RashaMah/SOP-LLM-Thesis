FINAL SOP EVALUATION JUDGE PROMPT — VERSION 1.0

You are an evaluator of generated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
for logistics and warehouse processes.

Your task is to evaluate ONE candidate SOP strictly against the supplied
SOURCE SCENARIO using the supplied eight-criterion evaluation rubric.

============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.


============================================================
SCORING PROCEDURE
============================================================

For EACH of the eight criteria:

1. Compare the candidate SOP directly with the source scenario.
2. Identify relevant evidence.
3. Identify substantive omissions, contradictions, unsupported additions,
   or ambiguities relevant to that criterion.
4. Assign exactly one INTEGER score from 1 to 5.
5. Provide a concise justification explaining the score.

Evaluate all eight criteria even if deficiencies overlap.

Do not calculate the total score. The experimental evaluation script will
calculate totals after the individual criterion scores have been recorded.

============================================================
MATERIAL TO EVALUATE
============================================================

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment

Scenario:
Current stock cannot satisfy all known future demand.

Objective:
Detect an inventory shortage and initiate an appropriate replenishment or stock-realignment response without incorrectly promising unavailable stock.

Operational information:
Inventory review shows that the quantity currently available for an item is lower than the quantity required for planned releases. Additional demand is already known. Warehouse personnel must verify the available stock and communicate the shortage to the responsible supply/planning function. Existing stock and planned releases must be considered when determining the next action. If stock from another location can be realigned, the transfer must be coordinated; otherwise replenishment must be initiated through the appropriate supply process.

Roles involved:
Inventory/Supply Officer, Warehouse Manager, Storekeeper, relevant planning or logistics function.

Constraints / decision conditions:
The procedure must not assume stock exists when it does not. Available stock must be verified before decisions are made. Pending requirements should not be silently cancelled or fulfilled with invented quantities.

Expected outcome:
The shortage is identified and documented, stakeholders are informed, and either replenishment or authorized stock realignment is initiated.


CANDIDATE SOP:

## 1. SOP Title
Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment Procedure

## 2. Purpose
To detect inventory shortages, verify available stock, and initiate appropriate replenishment or stock realignment actions to meet known future demand without promising unavailable stock.

## 3. Scope
This SOP applies to all warehouse operations where inventory levels are reviewed against known future demand, and replenishment or stock realignment actions are required.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities
- **Inventory/Supply Officer**: Responsible for reviewing inventory levels, identifying shortages, and coordinating replenishment or stock realignment actions.
- **Warehouse Manager**: Oversees the verification of available stock and coordinates with the Storekeeper for stock checks.
- **Storekeeper**: Responsible for verifying the physical stock levels as per the inventory records.
- **Relevant Planning or Logistics Function**: Receives notifications of inventory shortages and is responsible for processing replenishment or stock realignment requests.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information
- Current inventory levels for all items.
- Planned releases and additional known demand.
- Inventory requirements for planned releases.
- Information on stock availability at other locations (if applicable).

## 6. Expected Outputs
- Identification and documentation of inventory shortages.
- Notification to relevant stakeholders (Inventory/Supply Officer, Warehouse Manager, and relevant planning or logistics function) about the shortages.
- Initiation of either replenishment or stock realignment actions.

## 7. Procedure
1. **Review Inventory Levels**: The Inventory/Supply Officer reviews current inventory levels against the quantity required for planned releases and additional known demand to identify potential shortages.
   
2. **Identify Shortages**: If the available quantity is lower than the required quantity for an item, the Inventory/Supply Officer identifies it as a shortage.

3. **Verify Available Stock**: The Warehouse Manager instructs the Storekeeper to verify the physical stock level of the identified item(s) against the recorded inventory level.

4. **Confirm Shortage**: After verification by the Storekeeper, if a shortage is confirmed, the Warehouse Manager notifies the Inventory/Supply Officer.

5. **Check for Stock Realignment**: The Inventory/Supply Officer checks if stock is available at other locations that can be realigned to meet the shortage.

6. **Initiate Stock Realignment or Replenishment**:
   - If stock is available at another location, the Inventory/Supply Officer coordinates the transfer with the relevant logistics function.
   - If stock is not available at another location, the Inventory/Supply Officer initiates the replenishment process through the appropriate supply process.

7. **Notify Stakeholders**: The Inventory/Supply Officer notifies all relevant stakeholders (including the relevant planning or logistics function) about the shortage and the actions being taken.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling
- **Inaccurate Inventory Records**: If during verification, discrepancies are found between physical stock and recorded inventory levels, the Storekeeper and Warehouse Manager should investigate and correct the records accordingly before proceeding.
- **Unavailability of Stock for Realignment or Replenishment**: If stock cannot be realigned or replenished in time to meet the demand, the Inventory/Supply Officer should notify the relevant planning or logistics function to adjust plans accordingly.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria
The Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment process is considered complete when:
- The inventory shortage is identified and documented.
- The available stock is verified.
- Either stock realignment or replenishment action is initiated.
- All relevant stakeholders are notified about the shortage and the actions taken.
