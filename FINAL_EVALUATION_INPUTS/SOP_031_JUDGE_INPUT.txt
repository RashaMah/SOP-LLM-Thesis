============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment

Scenario:
Picking stock has fallen to a low level while reserve stock is available.

Objective:
Replenish the active picking location from warehouse reserve stock before it becomes insufficient for expected picking activity.

Operational information:
The warehouse uses separate active picking locations and reserve storage. During stock review, warehouse personnel determine that the quantity of an item in its picking location has become low. Additional quantity of the same item is available in reserve storage. The required stock must be transferred from reserve storage to the designated picking location and the inventory/location records must remain accurate after the movement.

Roles involved:
Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff, Warehouse or Store Manager.

CANDIDATE SOP:

# Standard Operating Procedure: Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment

## 1. SOP Title

**Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment**

## 2. Purpose

To replenish a low-stock active picking location using available reserve stock while ensuring:

- The correct product and stock batch are moved.
- Any applicable stock-rotation rule is maintained.
- Physical inventory remains aligned with inventory and location records.
- The picking location contains sufficient stock for continued picking operations.

## 3. Scope

This SOP applies when:

- An item’s quantity in its designated active picking location is identified as low.
- Additional quantity of the same item is available in reserve storage.
- Stock must be transferred from reserve storage to the designated picking location.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff

- Review stock in the active picking location.
- Confirm that matching stock is available in reserve storage.
- Identify the correct product and stock batch.
- Follow any applicable stock-rotation rule.
- Transfer the required stock from reserve storage to the designated picking location.
- Ensure the stock is placed in the correct location.
- Ensure inventory and location records accurately reflect the movement.
- Identify and report discrepancies or conditions that prevent correct replenishment.

### 4.2 Warehouse or Store Manager

- Oversee the replenishment and stock realignment process.
- Address exceptions, discrepancies, or deviations identified during the process.
- Confirm that unresolved inventory differences are not treated as completed movements.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information

The following information is required:

- Identity of the item requiring replenishment.
- Current physical quantity in the active picking location.
- Expected picking requirement sufficient to determine the replenishment need.
- Quantity of the same item available in reserve storage.
- Designated active picking location.
- Reserve storage location.
- Applicable stock batch information.
- Any applicable stock-rotation rule.
- Current inventory and location records.

## 6. Expected Outputs

- The correct product and stock batch are moved from reserve storage to the designated active picking location.
- The picking location contains sufficient stock for continued operations.
- Any applicable stock-rotation rule remains respected.
- Reserve and picking-location records reflect the stock movement.
- Physical and recorded quantities remain aligned.
- No unexplained inventory difference remains.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Identify the Replenishment Requirement

1. Review the stock quantity in the active picking location.
2. Confirm that the quantity has become low relative to expected picking activity.
3. Determine the quantity required to replenish the picking location sufficiently for continued operations.

### 7.2 Confirm Reserve Stock Availability

1. Identify reserve storage containing the same item.
2. Confirm that sufficient reserve stock is physically available for the required replenishment.
3. Compare the physical reserve stock with the applicable inventory and location records.
4. Do not proceed if the product, quantity, batch, or location cannot be confirmed.

### 7.3 Select the Correct Stock

1. Verify that the reserve stock is the same product required in the picking location.
2. Identify the stock batch to be transferred.
3. Confirm that the selected batch complies with any applicable stock-rotation rule.
4. Ensure that the intended quantity does not exceed the physically available reserve quantity.

### 7.4 Transfer the Stock

1. Remove the required quantity of the verified product and batch from reserve storage.
2. Maintain clear control of the product, batch, quantity, source location, and destination location during the movement.
3. Transfer the stock to the designated active picking location.
4. Place the stock in the correct picking location.
5. Arrange or position the stock so that any applicable stock-rotation rule continues to be respected.

### 7.5 Update Inventory and Location Records

1. Record the quantity removed from the reserve location.
2. Record the same quantity as added to the designated picking location.
3. Ensure the recorded product and batch match the stock physically moved.
4. Confirm that the transfer does not create an unexplained difference between reserve stock, picking stock, and the recorded inventory.

### 7.6 Verify the Completed Replenishment

1. Physically verify the stock in the picking location after replenishment.
2. Confirm that:
   - The product is correct.
   - The stock batch is correct.
   - The stock is in the designated picking location.
   - Any applicable stock-rotation rule has been maintained.
   - The picking location contains sufficient stock for continued operations.
3. Verify the remaining physical stock in reserve storage.
4. Compare the physical quantities in both locations with the updated inventory and location records.
5. Treat the replenishment as complete only when the physical and recorded quantities are aligned.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling

### 8.1 Incorrect Product or Batch

- Stop the movement if the product or batch does not match the replenishment requirement.
- Do not place incorrect stock in the picking location.
- Return any incorrectly selected stock to its correct reserve location if it can be done without creating an inventory or location discrepancy.
- Report the issue to the Warehouse or Store Manager if it cannot be resolved accurately.

### 8.2 Insufficient Reserve Stock

- Do not record or transfer more stock than is physically available.
- If available reserve stock is insufficient to meet the replenishment requirement, transfer only a quantity that can be accurately verified, provided the product, batch, location, and stock-rotation requirements remain satisfied.
- Report the remaining shortage to the Warehouse or Store Manager.
- Do not state that the picking location is sufficiently replenished unless it contains enough stock for continued operations.

### 8.3 Physical and Recorded Inventory Difference

- Stop the process if physical reserve stock or picking stock does not match the inventory or location records.
- Recheck the product, batch, quantities, source location, destination location, and movement record.
- Do not create an unsupported inventory adjustment or leave the difference unexplained.
- Report any unresolved discrepancy to the Warehouse or Store Manager.
- The movement must not be considered complete until physical and recorded quantities are aligned.

### 8.4 Stock-Rotation Conflict

- Do not complete the placement if the selected batch or stock arrangement would violate an applicable stock-rotation rule.
- Recheck the available batches and select or arrange stock in accordance with the applicable rule.
- Escalate the issue to the Warehouse or Store Manager if compliant replenishment cannot be completed.

### 8.5 Incorrect Destination Location

- Stop placement if the intended picking location cannot be confirmed.
- Do not place stock in an undesignated location.
- Confirm the correct destination before proceeding.
- Report the issue to the Warehouse or Store Manager if the correct location cannot be established.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria

The process is complete only when all of the following conditions are met:

- The correct product has been transferred.
- The correct stock batch has been used.
- The stock has been placed in the designated active picking location.
- The picking location contains sufficient stock for continued operations.
- Any applicable stock-rotation rule remains respected.
- The reserve and picking-location records accurately reflect the movement.
- Physical quantities match recorded quantities.
- No unexplained inventory or location difference remains.
