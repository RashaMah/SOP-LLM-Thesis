You are an evaluator of generated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
for logistics and warehouse processes.

Your task is to evaluate ONE candidate SOP strictly against the supplied
SOURCE SCENARIO using the supplied eight-criterion evaluation rubric.

============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.

SCORING PROCEDURE

For EACH of the eight criteria:

1. Compare the candidate SOP directly with the source scenario.

5. Provide a concise justification explaining the score.

Evaluate all eight criteria even if deficiencies overlap.

Do not calculate the total score. The experimental evaluation script will
calculate totals after the individual criterion scores have been recorded.

Process: Stock Transfer and Redistribution

Scenario:
One location holds surplus stock approaching a risk of expiration while another location faces insufficient stock.

Objective:
Realign inventory between locations to reduce the risk of expiry and prevent stockout.

Operational information:
Stock analysis identifies excess quantity of an item at one facility and insufficient quantity at another facility. The responsible Supply function determines that redistribution is required. # Standard Operating Procedure: Stock Transfer and Redistribution

## 1. SOP Title
Standard Operating Procedure: Stock Transfer and Redistribution

## 2. Purpose
The purpose of this procedure is to realign inventory between locations to reduce the risk of expiry and prevent stockout. This process ensures that surplus stock approaching a risk of expiration at one location is transferred to another location facing insufficient stock.

## 3. Scope
This SOP applies to situations where stock analysis identifies excess quantity of an item at one facility and insufficient quantity at another facility. It covers the identification, authorization, preparation, transport, receiving, and recording of stock movements between the source location and the receiving location.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities
The following roles are involved in the Stock Transfer and Redistribution process:

*   **Supply Officer:** Responsible for determining that redistribution is required based on stock analysis.
*   **Store Manager, Store Supervisor, Storekeeper, Store Helper/Warehouse Staff (Source Location):** Responsible for preparing the agreed quantity for collection or dispatch, including picking and packing. They must ensure the movement does not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
*   **Transport/3PL Personnel:** Responsible for the transport handover of the stock.
*   **Receiving Facility (including Store Manager, Store Supervisor, Storekeeper, Store Helper/Warehouse Staff):** Responsible for receiving the stock and recording the stock.
*   **All Involved Roles:** Responsible for ensuring that picking, packing, transport handover, receiving, and stock-record updates remain traceable throughout the transfer.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information
*   Stock analysis results identifying excess quantity at one facility and insufficient quantity at another.
*   Authorized decision confirming redistribution is required.
*   Agreed quantity for transfer based on stock information and the authorized decision.

## 6. Expected Outputs
*   Inventory redistributed from the surplus location to the shortage location.
*   Both locations' stock records correctly reflecting the movement.
*   Traceable records covering picking, packing, transport handover, receiving, and stock-record updates.

## 7. Procedure

**Step 1: Identification and Authorization**
1.  Conduct stock analysis to identify facilities with excess quantity of an item and facilities with insufficient quantity.
2.  The Supply function reviews the stock analysis.
3.  The Supply function determines that redistribution is required.
4.  An authorized decision is made regarding the redistribution.
5.  The quantity for redistribution is established based on stock information and the authorized decision.

**Step 2: Preparation at Source Location**
1.  The source location receives the instruction regarding the agreed quantity.
2.  Store Manager, Store Supervisor, Storekeeper, or Store Helper/Warehouse Staff at the source location prepare the agreed quantity for collection or dispatch.
3.  During preparation, verify that the movement will not create an unmanaged shortage at the source location.
4.  Perform picking and packing operations.
5.  Ensure all picking and packing activities are recorded to maintain traceability.

**Step 3: Transport and Handover**
1.  Arrange for transport via transport/3PL personnel.
2.  Execute the transport handover.
3.  Ensure the transport handover is documented to maintain traceability of the stock during transit.

**Step 4: Receiving at Destination**
1.  The receiving facility accepts the delivered stock.
2.  Verify the received stock against the agreed quantity.
3.  Record the stock upon receipt.
4.  Ensure receiving activities are documented to maintain traceability.

**Step 5: Stock Record Updates**
1.  Update stock records at the source location to reflect the outbound movement.
2.  Update stock records at the receiving location to reflect the inbound movement.
3.  Ensure stock-record updates are traceable and linked to the physical movement.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling
*   **Unmanaged Shortage Risk:** If the preparation of the agreed quantity indicates that the movement will create an unmanaged shortage at the source location, the process must be halted or adjusted to comply with the constraint that the movement should not create an unmanaged shortage.
*   **Loss of Traceability:** If any stage of the process (picking, packing, transport handover, receiving, or stock-record updates) fails to maintain traceability, the deviation must be addressed immediately to restore traceability before proceeding or finalizing the transaction.
*   **Discrepancy in Quantity:** If the received stock does not match the agreed quantity based on stock information and authorized decision, the discrepancy must be noted in the stock records to ensure accurate reflection of the movement.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria
The process is considered complete when:
1.  Inventory has been physically redistributed from the surplus location to the shortage location.
2.  Both locations' stock records correctly reflect the movement.
3.  Traceability is confirmed for picking, packing, transport handover, receiving, and stock-record updates.
