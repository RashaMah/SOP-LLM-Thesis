============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.


============================================================
SCORING PROCEDURE
============================================================

For EACH of the eight criteria:

1. Compare the candidate SOP directly with the source scenario.
2. Identify relevant evidence.
3. Identify substantive omissions, contradictions, unsupported additions,

4. Assign exactly one INTEGER score from 1 to 5.
5. Provide a concise justification explaining the score.

Evaluate all eight criteria even if deficiencies overlap.

Do not calculate the total score. The experimental evaluation script will
calculate totals after the individual criterion scores have been recorded.

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Goods Receipt / Receiving

Scenario: Incoming shipment contains damaged goods and a quantity discrepancy.

Objective:
Process an incoming shipment while correctly handling discrepancies identified during receiving.

Operational information:
A supplier delivery arrives with valid shipping documentation. During unloading and inspection, warehouse staff identify two problems: part of the shipment is visibly damaged and the physical quantity of one item does not match the quantity indicated in the documentation. The unaffected items are otherwise suitable for receipt. The discrepancy and damage must be documented and communicated to the responsible logistics/supply function. The affected items must not be treated as normal usable stock until a decision has been made.

Roles involved:
Warehouse Manager/Store Manager, Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff, inspection personnel, responsible Supply/Logistics function.

CANDIDATE SOP:

# Standard Operating Procedure: Goods Receipt with Damage and Quantity Discrepancy

## 1. Purpose

To define the process for receiving an incoming shipment when damaged goods and a quantity discrepancy are identified, ensuring that:

- Accepted goods are correctly received.
- Damaged or questionable goods are controlled and documented.
- Quantity discrepancies and damage are communicated to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
- Inventory records reflect only the accepted quantities and correct status.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to supplier deliveries with valid shipping documentation where, during unloading or inspection:

- Part of the shipment is visibly damaged.
- The physical quantity of an item does not match the quantity shown in the order or packing documentation.
- The remaining unaffected goods are otherwise suitable for receipt.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff

- Receive and unload the incoming shipment.
- Compare the physical delivery with the order and packing documentation.
- Identify quantity mismatches and visible damage.
- Clearly identify and separate damaged or questionable goods from normal available stock.
- Ensure unaffected goods remain distinguishable from affected goods.
- Support documentation of the receiving results.
- Prevent affected goods from being treated as normal usable stock before a decision is made.

### 3.2 Inspection Personnel

- Inspect the delivered goods.
- Confirm and document visible damage or questionable condition.
- Support the determination of which goods are suitable for normal receipt and which require controlled status.
- Ensure affected goods remain identified and separated pending a decision.

### 3.3 Warehouse Manager/Store Manager

- Oversee the receiving and discrepancy-handling process.
- Ensure all identified mismatches and damage are documented.
- Ensure affected goods are controlled and not entered or released as normal usable stock.
- Ensure the discrepancy is communicated to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
- Confirm that inventory records reflect only accepted quantities and the appropriate status of affected goods.

### 3.4 Responsible Supply/Logistics Function

- Receive notification and documentation concerning the damage and quantity discrepancy.
- Review the reported issues and make or coordinate the required decision concerning the affected goods.
- Communicate the disposition or further action for damaged or discrepant goods.

## 4. Required Inputs / Information

- Incoming supplier delivery.
- Valid shipping documentation.
- Applicable order documentation.
- Packing documentation.
- Physical quantities received.
- Inspection findings regarding condition and visible damage.
- Details of any difference between documented and physical quantities.

## 5. Expected Outputs

- Accepted goods correctly received as available stock.
- Damaged or questionable goods clearly identified, separated, and controlled.
- Quantity discrepancy documented.
- Damage documented.
- Discrepancy and damage communicated to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
- Inventory records reflecting only accepted quantities and the correct status.
- Affected goods held from normal use until a decision is made.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Receive and Review the Delivery

1. Receive the supplier delivery and its shipping documentation.
2. Confirm that valid shipping documentation accompanies the delivery.
3. Obtain the applicable order and packing documentation for comparison.

### 6.2 Unload and Inspect the Shipment

1. Unload the shipment.
2. Inspect the goods during unloading and receiving.
3. Identify any goods that are visibly damaged or otherwise questionable.
4. Keep affected goods distinguishable from unaffected goods throughout the receiving process.

### 6.3 Verify Physical Delivery

1. Compare the delivered items with the order and packing documentation.
2. Verify the physical quantity of each item against the documented quantity.
3. Record any mismatch between the physical delivery and the documentation.
4. For the item with a quantity discrepancy, document:
   - The item concerned.
   - The quantity indicated in the documentation.
   - The physical quantity received.
   - The resulting difference.

### 6.4 Control Damaged or Questionable Goods

1. Clearly identify all damaged or questionable goods.
2. Separate these goods from normal available stock.
3. Do not treat the affected goods as normal usable stock.
4. Maintain the separation and controlled status until the responsible Supply/Logistics function provides or coordinates a decision.

### 6.5 Process Unaffected Goods

1. Confirm that the unaffected goods are suitable for receipt.
2. Receive only the accepted quantities into normal available stock.
3. Ensure that damaged, questionable, or otherwise unaccepted quantities are not recorded as normal usable inventory.

### 6.6 Document the Findings

1. Document the visible damage identified during inspection.
2. Document the physical quantity discrepancy against the order and packing documentation.
3. Record which goods and quantities were:
   - Accepted.
   - Damaged or questionable.
   - Subject to a quantity discrepancy.
4. Ensure the documented receiving result corresponds to the physical condition and quantities identified.

### 6.7 Communicate and Escalate

1. Report the damage and quantity mismatch to the Warehouse Manager/Store Manager.
2. Communicate the documented damage and discrepancy to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
3. Maintain the affected goods in controlled, separated status while awaiting a decision.
4. Apply the decision communicated or coordinated by the responsible Supply/Logistics function.

### 6.8 Update Inventory Records

1. Update inventory records to reflect only the quantities accepted into normal usable stock.
2. Ensure affected goods are assigned a status that prevents them from being treated as normal available stock.
3. Verify that recorded quantities and statuses match the documented receiving result.

## 7. Exception and Deviation Handling

### 7.1 Visible Damage

- Clearly identify and separate visibly damaged goods.
- Do not release or record them as normal usable stock.
- Document the damage and report it to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
- Keep the goods controlled until a decision is made.

### 7.2 Quantity Discrepancy

- Compare the physical quantity with both the order and packing documentation.
- Document the documented quantity, physical quantity, and difference.
- Report the mismatch to the responsible Supply/Logistics function.
- Record only the accepted physical quantity and status in inventory.

### 7.3 Questionable Condition

- Treat goods with a questionable condition in the same controlled manner as visibly damaged goods.
- Identify and separate them from available stock.
- Do not treat them as normal usable stock until a decision is made.

### 7.4 Documentation or Inventory Conflict

If the documented receiving result does not match the physical goods or inventory record:

- Do not finalize the affected quantity as normal usable stock.
- Recheck the physical delivery against the order and packing documentation.
- Correct the receiving information so that inventory reflects only the accepted quantities and appropriate statuses.
- Escalate any unresolved mismatch to the Warehouse Manager/Store Manager and responsible Supply/Logistics function.

## 8. Process Completion Criteria

The receiving process is complete when:

- The physical delivery has been compared with the order and packing documentation.
- All damage and quantity discrepancies have been documented.
- All mismatches have been reported.
- The responsible Supply/Logistics function has been notified.
- Unaffected goods have been correctly received.
- Damaged or questionable goods are clearly identified, separated, and prevented from normal use pending a decision.
- Inventory records reflect only accepted quantities and the correct status of affected goods.
- Any decision provided for the affected goods has been applied, or the goods remain controlled pending that decision.
