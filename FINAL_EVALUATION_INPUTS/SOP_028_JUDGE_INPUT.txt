FINAL SOP EVALUATION JUDGE PROMPT — VERSION 1.0

You are an evaluator of generated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
for logistics and warehouse processes.

Your task is to evaluate ONE candidate SOP strictly against the supplied
SOURCE SCENARIO using the supplied eight-criterion evaluation rubric.

============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.


============================================================
SCORING PROCEDURE
============================================================

For EACH of the eight criteria:

1. Compare the candidate SOP directly with the source scenario.
2. Identify relevant evidence.
3. Identify substantive omissions, contradictions, unsupported additions,
   or ambiguities relevant to that criterion.
4. Assign exactly one INTEGER score from 1 to 5.
5. Provide a concise justification explaining the score.

Evaluate all eight criteria even if deficiencies overlap.

Do not calculate the total score. The experimental evaluation script will
calculate totals after the individual criterion scores have been recorded.

MATERIAL TO EVALUATE
============================================================

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment

Scenario:
Picking stock has fallen to a low level while reserve stock is available.

Objective:
Replenish the active picking location from warehouse reserve stock before it becomes insufficient for expected picking activity.

Operational information:
The warehouse uses separate active picking locations and reserve storage. During stock review, warehouse personnel determine that the quantity of an item in its picking location has become low. Additional quantity of the same item is available in reserve storage. The required stock must be transferred from reserve storage to the designated picking location and the inventory/location records must remain accurate after the movement.

Roles involved:
Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff, Warehouse or Store Manager.

Constraints / decision conditions:
The correct product and stock batch must be used. The movement must not create an unexplained difference between physical and recorded inventory. Any applicable stock-rotation rule must continue to be respected.

Expected outcome:
The picking location contains sufficient stock for continued operations and both physical and recorded quantities remain aligned.


CANDIDATE SOP:

## 1. SOP Title
Inventory Replenishment and Stock Realignment Procedure

## 2. Purpose
To ensure that the active picking locations in the warehouse are replenished with the correct stock from reserve storage, maintaining sufficient stock levels for picking operations and ensuring that physical and recorded inventory quantities remain accurate.

## 3. Scope
This SOP applies to all warehouse operations involving the replenishment of active picking locations from reserve storage, managed by the Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff under the supervision of the Warehouse or Store Manager.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities
- **Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff:** Responsible for identifying the need for replenishment, conducting the stock transfer from reserve storage to the active picking location, and updating inventory/location records.
- **Warehouse or Store Manager:** Oversees the replenishment process, ensures adherence to this SOP, and verifies the accuracy of inventory/location records after the transfer.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information
- Current quantity of stock in the active picking location.
- Quantity and location of the same item in reserve storage.
- Stock batch information for the item in both the picking location and reserve storage.
- Inventory/location records for the item.

## 6. Expected Outputs
- The active picking location is replenished with the correct stock.
- Inventory/location records are updated to reflect the stock movement.
- Physical and recorded quantities of the item are aligned.

## 7. Procedure
1. **Identify the Need for Replenishment:** Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff review stock levels in active picking locations and identify items that have fallen to a low level.
2. **Verify Availability of Reserve Stock:** Check if the same item is available in reserve storage and confirm the quantity and batch information.
3. **Plan the Replenishment:** Determine the quantity needed to replenish the picking location to the required level, ensuring not to overstock. Verify that the batch to be transferred is correct and adheres to any applicable stock rotation rules (e.g., First-In-First-Out).
4. **Transfer Stock:** Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff retrieve the required quantity from reserve storage and transfer it to the designated picking location.
5. **Update Inventory/Location Records:** After the transfer, update the inventory/location records to reflect the movement of stock from reserve storage to the picking location. Ensure that the records accurately show the new quantities in both locations.
6. **Verify the Transfer:** Warehouse or Store Manager verifies that the transfer was conducted correctly, the picking location is adequately stocked, and the inventory/location records are accurate.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling
- **Incorrect Stock or Batch:** If the wrong stock or batch is identified during or after the transfer, the Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff must immediately notify the Warehouse or Store Manager. The stock must be returned to its original location, and the inventory records corrected. The replenishment process should be restarted with the correct stock.
- **Inventory Discrepancy:** If a discrepancy between physical and recorded inventory is discovered during or after the transfer, the Storekeeper/Warehouse Staff must halt the process and notify the Warehouse or Store Manager. An investigation should be conducted to identify the cause, and corrective actions taken to resolve the discrepancy before proceeding.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria
The inventory replenishment and stock realignment process is considered complete when:
- The active picking location has been replenished with the correct stock to a sufficient level.
- Inventory/location records have been accurately updated to reflect the stock movement.
- Verification by the Warehouse or Store Manager confirms that the process was conducted correctly and that physical and recorded quantities are aligned.
