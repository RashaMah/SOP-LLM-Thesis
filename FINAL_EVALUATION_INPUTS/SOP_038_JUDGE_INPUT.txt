FINAL SOP EVALUATION JUDGE PROMPT — VERSION 1.0

You are an evaluator of generated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
for logistics and warehouse processes.

Your task is to evaluate ONE candidate SOP strictly against the supplied
SOURCE SCENARIO using the supplied eight-criterion evaluation rubric.

============================================================
EVALUATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

1. Treat the SOURCE SCENARIO as the sole factual reference.

2. Do not use external operational knowledge to impose requirements that
   are absent from the source scenario.

3. Do not reward the candidate SOP for adding plausible but unsupported
   operational details.

4. Do not penalize the candidate SOP for omitting information that was
   never provided in the source scenario.

5. Evaluate only the candidate SOP provided.

6. Do not infer, identify, or speculate about which language model
   generated the candidate SOP.

7. Score each criterion independently.

8. Base each score on concrete comparison between the SOURCE SCENARIO and
   the CANDIDATE SOP.

9. Do not reward verbosity, document length, sophisticated formatting,
   or writing style unless these materially improve the criterion being
   evaluated.

10. Reasonable paraphrasing is acceptable when the operational meaning
    of the source scenario is preserved.

11. Do not compare the candidate SOP with other candidate SOPs.

12. Do not calculate or provide an overall score or model ranking.


============================================================
C1 — COMPLETENESS
============================================================

Evaluate whether all operationally relevant information explicitly
contained in the source scenario is represented in the SOP, including
required activities, information, roles, conditions, and expected outcomes.

Do not penalize information that was not provided in the source scenario.


============================================================
C2 — CORRECT SEQUENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether procedural activities follow the operational dependencies
and sequence specified or implied by the source scenario.

Penalize contradictory, illogical, or operationally incorrect ordering.


============================================================
C3 — ROLE CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether responsibilities are clearly and consistently assigned
to roles provided in the source scenario.

Penalize ambiguous responsibility, incorrect role assignment, or
unsupported new roles when these materially affect the procedure.


============================================================
C4 — INPUT / OUTPUT CLARITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether required inputs and information, as well as expected
outputs and results, are clearly identifiable and consistent with the
source scenario.


============================================================
C5 — EXCEPTION HANDLING
============================================================

Evaluate whether exceptions, deviations, abnormal conditions, and
decision points contained in the source scenario are correctly identified
and handled.

Do not penalize the SOP merely because the source scenario itself contains
few exceptions.


============================================================
C6 — CONSTRAINT ADHERENCE
============================================================

Evaluate whether explicit constraints and decision conditions contained
in the source scenario are preserved and respected throughout the SOP.


============================================================
C7 — GROUNDEDNESS / NO HALLUCINATION
============================================================

Evaluate whether operational claims in the SOP remain grounded in the
source scenario.

Penalize invented systems, roles, requirements, thresholds, documents,
technologies, approvals, rules, or other material operational details
that are unsupported by the source scenario.

Do not penalize harmless paraphrasing, organizational formatting, or
wording changes that preserve the original operational meaning.


============================================================
C8 — USABILITY
============================================================

Evaluate whether the SOP is clear, structured, internally consistent,
unambiguous, and actionable for the intended warehouse or logistics user,
while remaining faithful to the source scenario.


============================================================
SCORING SCALE
============================================================

5 — FULLY SATISFIES
The SOP fully satisfies the criterion. No substantive deficiency is
identifiable.

4 — GOOD
The SOP satisfies the criterion with only a minor omission, ambiguity,
or weakness that does not materially affect the procedure.

3 — ADEQUATE
The SOP partially satisfies the criterion. One meaningful deficiency or
several minor deficiencies are present, but the SOP remains substantially
correct and usable on this dimension.

2 — POOR
Major deficiencies are present. Important information, logic, or
requirements are missing, unclear, or incorrect and materially affect
this dimension.

1 — VERY POOR
The SOP fundamentally fails the criterion, substantially contradicts the
source scenario, or omits or incorrectly handles most essential elements
relevant to the criterion.


============================================================
SCORING PROCEDURE
============================================================

For EACH of the eight criteria:

1. Compare the candidate SOP directly with the source scenario.
2. Identify relevant evidence.
3. Identify substantive omissions, contradictions, unsupported additions,
   or ambiguities relevant to that criterion.
4. Assign exactly one INTEGER score from 1 to 5.
5. Provide a concise justification explaining the score.

Evaluate all eight criteria even if deficiencies overlap.

Do not calculate the total score. The experimental evaluation script will
calculate totals after the individual criterion scores have been recorded.

============================================================
MATERIAL TO EVALUATE
============================================================

SOURCE SCENARIO:

Process: Order Picking

Scenario:
A warehouse operator must prepare a normal multi-item picking wave.

Objective:
Pick all required products and quantities accurately from their assigned warehouse locations and transfer the completed pick for the next fulfilment stage.

Operational information:
An approved picking instruction contains multiple product references, required quantities and corresponding storage locations. One operator or picking team is assigned to the picking task. The warehouse uses identified storage locations and the order may contain items from several locations. Items must be picked according to the authorized picking information and transferred to the designated staging/packing area after completion.

Roles involved:
Storekeeper/Picker, Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.

Constraints / decision conditions:
Only the product references and quantities stated in the picking instruction may be picked. Each picked line must be checked against the instruction. Inventory/location records must remain accurate after the physical movement.

Expected outcome:
All available requested items are picked accurately, checked and transferred to the next order-fulfilment stage.


CANDIDATE SOP:

# 1. SOP Title

**Normal Multi-Item Order Picking**

# 2. Purpose

To ensure that all available products required by an approved multi-item picking instruction are accurately picked from their assigned warehouse locations, checked, and transferred to the designated staging/packing area for the next fulfilment stage.

# 3. Scope

This SOP applies to normal multi-item picking waves assigned to one Storekeeper/Picker or picking team. It covers:

- Review of the approved picking instruction.
- Picking from one or more identified storage locations.
- Verification of each picked line.
- Maintenance of accurate inventory/location records.
- Transfer of the completed pick to the designated staging/packing area.

# 4. Roles and Responsibilities

## 4.1 Storekeeper/Picker

- Review the approved picking instruction.
- Pick only the authorized product references and quantities.
- Pick products from the corresponding assigned storage locations.
- Check each picked line against the picking instruction.
- Ensure physical movements are reflected accurately in inventory/location records.
- Transfer the completed pick to the designated staging/packing area.
- Report shortages, product discrepancies, location discrepancies, or other deviations to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.

## 4.2 Warehouse Supervisor or Manager

- Oversee the picking process.
- Address reported shortages, discrepancies, or deviations.
- Provide direction when the approved picking instruction cannot be completed as stated.

# 5. Required Inputs / Information

- An approved picking instruction containing:
  - Multiple product references.
  - Required quantity for each product reference.
  - Corresponding storage location for each product reference.
- Assignment of the picking task to one Storekeeper/Picker or picking team.
- Identification of the designated staging/packing area.

# 6. Expected Outputs

- All available requested items picked accurately.
- Each picked line checked against the approved picking instruction.
- No unauthorized product references or quantities included.
- Accurate inventory/location records following physical movement.
- Completed pick transferred to the designated staging/packing area for the next fulfilment stage.
- Any shortage or discrepancy reported for resolution.

# 7. Procedure

## 7.1 Review the Picking Instruction

1. Obtain the approved picking instruction assigned to the Storekeeper/Picker or picking team.
2. Review all listed product references, required quantities, and corresponding storage locations.
3. Confirm that the instruction covers a multi-item picking wave and may require picking from several storage locations.
4. Do not pick any product that is not listed on the approved picking instruction.

## 7.2 Proceed to the Assigned Locations

1. Follow the storage locations stated in the approved picking instruction.
2. At each location, compare the identified location with the location stated for the required product reference.
3. If the location does not correspond with the instruction, stop processing the affected line and follow the exception procedure.

## 7.3 Pick the Products

For each line on the approved picking instruction:

1. Identify the required product reference at the stated storage location.
2. Compare the product reference with the reference shown on the picking instruction.
3. Determine the required quantity.
4. Pick only the quantity authorized by the picking instruction.
5. Do not substitute another product reference or pick an additional quantity.
6. Keep the picked items together as part of the assigned picking task.

## 7.4 Check Each Picked Line

1. Check every picked line against the approved picking instruction.
2. Verify:
   - Product reference.
   - Picked quantity.
   - Assigned storage location.
3. Correct any picking error before completing the task, provided the correction remains consistent with the approved instruction.
4. If the required product or quantity is unavailable, follow the exception procedure.

## 7.5 Maintain Inventory and Location Accuracy

1. Ensure inventory/location records remain accurate after each physical movement.
2. Confirm that the recorded movement corresponds to:
   - The product reference physically picked.
   - The quantity physically picked.
   - The storage location from which it was picked.
3. Report any difference between the physical stock or location and the applicable records to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.

## 7.6 Complete the Picking Task

1. Review the full picking instruction after all listed lines have been processed.
2. Confirm that:
   - Every line has been checked.
   - All available requested items have been picked.
   - Only authorized products and quantities have been included.
   - Any unavailable quantity or discrepancy has been reported.
   - Inventory/location records remain accurate after the movement.

## 7.7 Transfer the Completed Pick

1. Transfer the completed pick to the designated staging/packing area.
2. Place the picked items in the area intended for the next order-fulfilment stage.
3. Ensure the completed pick remains associated with the applicable picking instruction.

# 8. Exception and Deviation Handling

## 8.1 Product Not Available or Insufficient Quantity

1. Do not replace the unavailable product with another product reference.
2. Pick only the available quantity if it is part of the requested product and does not exceed the authorized quantity.
3. Report the unavailable or short quantity to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.
4. Ensure the picked quantity and inventory/location records reflect the actual physical movement.
5. Follow the direction of the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager for the unresolved portion of the instruction.

## 8.2 Product Reference Discrepancy

If the product at the stated location does not match the instructed product reference:

1. Do not pick the non-matching product.
2. Stop processing the affected line.
3. Report the discrepancy to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.
4. Do not substitute another product unless the authorized picking information is changed accordingly.

## 8.3 Location Discrepancy

If the product is not found at the stated storage location, or the location information appears incorrect:

1. Do not pick from an unauthorized location based on assumption.
2. Report the location discrepancy to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.
3. Ensure that any subsequent physical movement remains consistent with authorized picking information and accurate inventory/location records.

## 8.4 Quantity Discrepancy or Overpick

If more than the authorized quantity has been picked:

1. Do not transfer the excess quantity to the staging/packing area as part of the order.
2. Correct the physical quantity so it matches the picking instruction.
3. Ensure inventory/location records accurately reflect the final physical movement.
4. Report any discrepancy that cannot be corrected to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.

## 8.5 Inventory or Location Record Discrepancy

1. Compare the physical product, quantity, and location with the applicable records.
2. Report any inconsistency to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.
3. Do not treat the picking task as fully complete until the physical movement is accurately reflected in the inventory/location records.

# 9. Process Completion Criteria

The order-picking process is complete when:

- Every line on the approved picking instruction has been processed and checked.
- All available requested products and quantities have been accurately picked.
- No unauthorized product reference or quantity is included.
- Any shortage or discrepancy has been reported to the Warehouse Supervisor or Manager.
- Inventory/location records accurately reflect the physical movement.
- The completed pick has been transferred to the designated staging/packing area for the next fulfilment stage.
