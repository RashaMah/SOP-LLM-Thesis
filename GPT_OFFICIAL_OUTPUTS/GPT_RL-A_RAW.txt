# Standard Operating Procedure: Reverse Logistics and Damaged Goods Handling

## 1. SOP Title

**Reverse Logistics and Damaged Goods Handling for Non-Conforming Warehouse Stock**

## 2. Purpose

To ensure that goods identified as non-conforming due to a documented quality problem are:

- Removed from usable inventory;
- Prevented from being picked for normal use;
- Clearly isolated and kept traceable;
- Correctly reflected in stock records; and
- Prepared for authorized return or other coordinated disposition.

## 3. Scope

This SOP applies to goods already held in the warehouse that are identified as unsuitable for normal use because of a documented quality problem.

It covers the process from identification of the affected stored batch through isolation, stock record update, preparation, and authorized removal from the warehouse.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Supply Officer/Logistics Function

- Coordinate the further return or disposition process.
- Ensure that removal from the warehouse is authorized and documented.
- Coordinate the supporting documentation required to accompany the goods when they leave the warehouse.

### 4.2 Warehouse or Store Manager

- Oversee the identification, removal, and isolation of affected stock.
- Ensure affected goods are not available for normal picking.
- Ensure stock records are updated.
- Ensure goods leave the warehouse only through an authorized and documented process.

### 4.3 Storekeeper

- Identify and verify the affected stock and quantity.
- Remove the affected quantity from normal usable stock.
- Place the goods in a clearly separated holding area.
- Update the relevant stock records while preserving identity and quantity traceability.
- Assist in preparing the goods for authorized return or disposition.

### 4.4 Warehouse Staff

- Support identification, removal, handling, and transfer of affected goods.
- Prevent affected goods from being returned to normal usable inventory or selected for normal picking.
- Maintain separation and traceability during handling.

## 5. Required Inputs / Information

The process requires:

- Identification of the stored batch affected by the quality problem;
- Documentation of the quality problem;
- Information needed to identify the affected goods;
- Confirmation of the affected quantity;
- Relevant stock records;
- Authorization and supporting documentation for return or disposition before the goods leave the warehouse.

## 6. Expected Outputs

- Affected goods removed from normal usable inventory;
- Affected goods placed in a clearly separated holding area;
- Affected identity and quantity maintained as traceable;
- Relevant stock records updated;
- Affected goods prevented from normal picking;
- Return or disposition coordinated by the Supply Officer/Logistics function;
- Required supporting documentation prepared to accompany the goods; and
- Goods released from the warehouse only through an authorized and documented process.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Confirm the Non-Conforming Stock

1. Review the documented quality problem affecting the stored batch.
2. Identify the goods and batch covered by the documented problem.
3. Determine and verify the affected quantity.
4. Maintain a clear link between the documented quality problem and the identity and quantity of the affected goods.

### 7.2 Remove Goods from Usable Inventory

1. Locate the affected goods in the warehouse.
2. Remove the confirmed affected quantity from normal usable stock.
3. Ensure that the affected goods are no longer available for normal picking.
4. Keep the affected goods identifiable and traceable throughout removal and handling.

### 7.3 Isolate the Affected Goods

1. Transfer the affected goods to a clearly separated holding area.
2. Keep the goods separated from normal available inventory.
3. Maintain the identity and quantity of the affected stock while it is held.
4. Do not return the goods to normal usable inventory unless handled through an authorized process.

### 7.4 Update Stock Records

1. Update the relevant stock records to reflect the removal of the affected quantity from normal usable stock.
2. Record the affected goods in a manner that preserves their identity and quantity traceability.
3. Verify that warehouse records no longer show the affected goods as available for normal picking.

### 7.5 Coordinate Return or Disposition

1. Notify or engage the responsible Supply Officer/Logistics function.
2. Provide the available information concerning:
   - The identity of the affected goods;
   - The affected quantity;
   - The documented quality problem; and
   - The location of the isolated goods.
3. The Supply Officer/Logistics function shall coordinate the further return or disposition process.
4. Keep the goods in the separated holding area until the authorized process is ready.

### 7.6 Prepare for Warehouse Release

1. Confirm that the return or disposition is authorized.
2. Prepare the affected goods for release while maintaining their identity, quantity, and separation from usable inventory.
3. Ensure that all required supporting documentation is available.
4. Ensure the required supporting documentation accompanies the goods when they leave the warehouse.
5. Release the goods only through the authorized and documented process.
6. Update relevant stock records to reflect the goods leaving the warehouse.

## 8. Exception and Deviation Handling

### 8.1 Affected Quantity Cannot Be Confirmed

- Do not release the goods for normal picking.
- Keep potentially affected goods separated from usable inventory.
- Escalate the discrepancy to the Warehouse or Store Manager and the Supply Officer/Logistics function.
- Do not proceed with warehouse release until the identity and quantity are traceable and the process is authorized.

### 8.2 Goods Cannot Be Clearly Identified

- Prevent the goods from remaining available for normal picking.
- Place the potentially affected goods in the clearly separated holding area.
- Maintain all available identifying information.
- Refer the issue to the Warehouse or Store Manager and the Supply Officer/Logistics function for coordinated handling.

### 8.3 Stock Record Discrepancy

- Keep the affected goods isolated.
- Do not treat the goods as normal available inventory.
- Verify the physical identity and quantity against the relevant stock records.
- Escalate unresolved discrepancies to the Warehouse or Store Manager and the Supply Officer/Logistics function.

### 8.4 Authorization or Documentation Is Missing

- Do not allow the goods to leave the warehouse.
- Retain the goods in the clearly separated holding area.
- Refer the issue to the Supply Officer/Logistics function for coordination.
- Release the goods only after the process is authorized and the required supporting documentation is available.

### 8.5 Separation Is Compromised

- Stop handling activities that could mix the affected goods with usable inventory.
- Re-establish clear separation immediately.
- Reconfirm the identity and quantity of the affected goods.
- Update records if any discrepancy is identified.

## 9. Process Completion Criteria

The process is complete when:

- The affected goods have been identified and the affected quantity confirmed;
- The goods have been removed from normal usable stock;
- The goods are not available for normal picking;
- The goods have been held in a clearly separated area until authorized release;
- Their identity and quantity have remained traceable;
- Relevant stock records have been updated;
- The Supply Officer/Logistics function has coordinated the return or disposition;
- The warehouse release has been authorized and documented;
- Required supporting documentation accompanies the goods; and
- The goods have left the warehouse through the authorized process without contaminating normal available inventory.
